crossfeed privacy policy

applies to Crossfeed and Crossfeed Player · last updated 12 August 2026

Crossfeed is built so that almost nothing leaves your phone. This page says exactly what stays, what goes, and how to get rid of it.

the short version

Your listening history and your music library never leave your device. There are no adverts, no analytics, no trackers and no third-party SDKs of any kind.

One feature, auxshare, sends data to a server, and only after you switch it on. Nothing else does.

what stays on your phone

  • Your listening history. Every play, with the track, artist, album, app and how long you listened. Stored in a private database only Crossfeed can read.
  • Your music library. Titles, artists, albums and file locations, read from Android's media store so the app can list and play them.
  • Your settings, including which apps you route links to.

None of this is transmitted anywhere. You can erase the history at any time from the listening tab, and uninstalling the app deletes all of it.

notification access

The listening diary works by reading the now playing card that music apps publish: track, artist, album and position. This needs Android's notification access permission, which you grant yourself and can revoke at any time.

Crossfeed does not read, store or transmit the contents of any other notification. Notifications from messaging, email, banking or any other app are ignored entirely.

what auxshare sends, if you turn it on

Auxshare is off until you create a handle and move the sharing switches. It has no email address, no password and no real name.

services crossfeed talks to

To fill in lyrics, credits and cover art, the app queries these services with a song title and artist. No account identifier, handle or device identifier is sent with those requests, and none of them are told who is asking.

  • LRCLIB for synced lyrics
  • MusicBrainz for credits
  • Apple iTunes Search for catalogue matches and cover art

The auxshare server is operated by the developer on Cloudflare, in the United States. All traffic uses HTTPS.

what leaves the phone, and when

Your listening history is never uploaded. Some features do ask other services a question, and a question names the track it is about, so each one is listed here and each can be turned off.

  • Genre lookups. Off unless you turn them on. Sends a title and artist to Apple so the genres chart can fill in.
  • Cover art and cross service links. Sends a title and artist to Apple when a song needs a sleeve, or when you ask for a link to the same song elsewhere.
  • Music played in a browser. Off unless you turn it on. Sends the title a browser or video app is publishing, so it can be checked against the catalogue before it counts. Titles that turn out not to be music are discarded.
  • Translation. Runs entirely on the phone. A language pack is downloaded once from Google, which is a file request and carries nothing about you or what you are playing. Lyrics are never sent anywhere.

the update notice

The app asks the crossfeed server whether there is a notice to show, such as a new version being available. The answer is usually nothing. The request carries no identifier and works without an account.

how long it is kept

Posts are deleted after a year, and reactions after thirty days. Saves and connections stay until you remove them or wipe your account.

deleting your data

In the app: auxshare → wipe my account. That removes your handle, posts, saves, reactions and every connection from the server, permanently and immediately.

If you no longer have the app installed, see the account deletion page.

children

Crossfeed is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and any change that widens what is collected will be announced in the app before it takes effect.
