the diary

it counts, whoever is playing

crossfeed reads the now playing card that every music app on android already publishes. so a song counts the same whether it came from spotify, apple music, youtube music in a browser tab, or a file sitting on your phone.

nothing is uploaded to make that work. there is no account, no scrobbling service in the middle, and no third party holding a copy of what you listen to.

video apps and browsers are refused by default. turn them on and a video still has to prove it is music — the artist has to be someone the catalogue actually lists — so lectures and clips stay out of your history.

one player

drive any app from here

whatever is playing appears at the top of the page and in the bar above the tabs — with a scrubber, a play button and skip. pause apple music from inside crossfeed and apple music pauses.

it works because android hands out the controls, not because those apps agreed to anything. no api key, no developer account, no permission from spotify.

links

a link for whoever you are sending it to

share any music link into crossfeed and it finds the same song everywhere else. open it in the app you use, or copy the link for the service your friend uses.

share a playlist and it opens up into its songs, each one shareable in its own right. the search on the player page reaches the whole catalogue too, and tags the results already on your phone so you can play them on the spot.

sing along

the words, and what they mean

lyrics scroll in time with the song. for anything not in your own language there is a switch that puts the meaning under each line as you go — no second screen, no leaving the page.

translation runs on the phone. a language pack is fetched once, and after that it works with no connection at all. the words themselves are never sent anywhere.

your history

take it with you, and ask it anything

every play, every total, every finish rate, written to a spreadsheet you own. eleven sheets, saved straight to downloads.

which makes it something you can hand to any ai you like and simply ask — what did i play to death in march, what did i abandon halfway, what have i not touched since last winter. the charts in the app are a starting point, not the limit of what your own history can tell you.

it imports back too, so moving to a new phone costs you nothing.

auxshare

a small room, not an audience

share what you are playing with the handful of people you actually swap music with. see theirs as it happens, react to it, reply with a song of your own.

it is off until you turn it on, it is people you added by handle and nobody else, and it can be paused for an hour or a day when you would rather not be watched. your diary stays on your phone either way — only what you choose to share ever leaves.

the settings

nothing on unless you say so

every permission is optional and every one says what it unlocks. they are checked again each time you open the app, so if you take one back it shows here.

sharing, location, browser capture, translation — all off or on by your hand, none of them required to keep a diary.
